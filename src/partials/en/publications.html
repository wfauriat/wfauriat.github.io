The "frame" problem in quantitative practice

▾

Fauriat, W. (2026). The frame problem in quantitative practice: ontological uncertainty and epistemic humility in an age of automated inference. Preprint on arXiv

A synthetic, application-oriented review arguing that quantitative practice across statistics, engineering, and machine learning operates under three distinct categories of uncertainty — aleatory, epistemic, and frame (or ontological). The third, the residue of finite specification, is structurally invisible to formal analysis within the chosen frame and is the locus of most consequential failures. The paper traces why the limit applies equally to deductive and inductive reasoning, why no meta-level procedure dissolves the regress, and why current conditions of automated inference make epistemic humility more, not less, important.

Key Contributions

  • Cross-disciplinary unification of the frame problem across statistics, engineering UQ, AI, and ML literatures
  • Three-category taxonomy of uncertainty (aleatory / epistemic / frame) and the structural invisibility of the third
  • Targeted analysis for five practices — engineer, statistician, mathematician, ML practitioner, non-specialist recipient
  • Epistemic humility framed as a practical disposition with implications for builders, decision-makers, institutions, and pedagogy

Value of information in maintenance

▾

Fauriat, W., & Zio, E. (2020). Optimization of an aperiodic sequential inspection and condition-based maintenance policy driven by value of information. Reliability Engineering & System Safety, 204, 107133.

Decision-theoretic framework for optimal maintenance scheduling using Value of Information (VoI). Addresses when and how to inspect degrading systems by quantifying the economic benefit of condition monitoring versus the cost of inspections and failures.

Key Contributions

  • VoI-driven framework for aperiodic inspection scheduling optimization
  • Bayesian updating of degradation models with sequential inspection data
  • Trade-off analysis between inspection costs, failure risks, and information gain
  • Application to condition-based maintenance in industrial systems

Estimation of road profile variability

▾

Fauriat, W., Mattrand, C., Gayton, N., Beakou, A., & Cembrzynski, T. (2016). Estimation of road profile variability from measured vehicle responses. Vehicle System Dynamics, 54(5), 585-605.

Data processing algorithm for estimating road profiles from vehicle dynamic responses. Addresses the inverse problem of inferring road excitation from measured vehicle displacement and acceleration using Kalman filtering in a stochastic framework.

Key Contributions

  • Kalman filtering-based algorithm for road profile reconstruction from vehicle sensors
  • Stochastic inverse problem formulation for uncertainty quantification
  • Validation on experimental vehicle dynamics measurements
  • Application to automotive durability assessment and road characterization

AK-SYS: system reliability method

▾

Fauriat, W., & Gayton, N. (2014). AK-SYS: an adaptation of the AK-MCS method for system reliability. Reliability Engineering & System Safety, 123, 137-144.

Novel adaptive Kriging method for efficient system reliability analysis. Extends the AK-MCS approach to handle series/parallel systems and complex failure configurations through intelligent sampling strategies.

Key Contributions

  • Adaptive enrichment strategy for Kriging surrogate models in system reliability
  • Efficient rare event probability estimation for series and parallel systems
  • Significant reduction in computational cost compared to Monte Carlo methods
  • Demonstrated on structural mechanics and engineering reliability problems

A discourse on the method in the 21st century

▾

Fauriat, W. (2022). Un Discours de la Méthode au XXIe Siècle. 292 pages. (Book — published in French)

An interdisciplinary essay on rationality, probability, decision-making and the limits of algorithmic reasoning. Built around a fictional Socratic framing — Professor Seracost and six students debating their university's supercalculator — the book bridges philosophy, mathematics, engineering, economics and computer science to argue that rationality is necessarily subjective, that risk is observer-relative, and that formal machines inherit (not transcend) our epistemic limitations.

Key Themes

  • Subjective Bayesianism (de Finetti tradition) as a foundation for rational decision under uncertainty
  • Pedagogical buildup from everyday choices to formal probability and decision theory
  • Manifesto on the limits of algorithmic rationality — "formaliser, c'est déjà choisir"
  • Critical reflection (written pre-LLM, 2020-2022) on conflating computational power with intelligence
